ਅੱਜ ਦਾ ਵਿਚਾਰ: a card that changes every day, and that people can make their own

Today the picture is the same cream folio every day; only the line changes. The proposal: the card dresses itself for the day (Nanakshahi date, the day's event, a Shaheedi or Prakash look), and the sender can pick a style, a size, a greeting and add their own name. The Gurbani line and its meaning always come from content.db and can never be edited.

Every choice laid out in tabs (Style, Words for greeting, meaning and from, Size). Thorough, but it feels like a tool rather than a quick share.

  • What makes it different every day without anyone choosing: the line (already), the Nanakshahi date, the day's event, and on event days the Shaheedi or Prakash style. The person's last choice of style and size is remembered.
  • Greetings follow the day. A Shaheedi day never offers "ਵਧਾਈਆਂ" (congratulations); it offers ਸ਼ਹੀਦਾਂ ਨੂੰ ਕੋਟਿ ਕੋਟਿ ਪ੍ਰਣਾਮ. A Prakash Purab offers ਲੱਖ ਲੱਖ ਵਧਾਈਆਂ. Greetings are fixed choices in pa, hi and en, never typed by the user, so nothing sits next to Gurbani that we have not checked. The Punjabi wording here is my draft and wants your ear.
  • Only "From" is free text (30 characters, on the phone only). It sits at the foot, away from the line.
  • The Photo style uses assets/vichar/dawn.jpg, one of your five pictures. Other pictures would need their rights checked first (CLAUDE.md notes this for the night painting).
  • Event names are English for now: 137 of the 147 calendar events have no Punjabi name in content.db yet. Filling them is content work, needed before this ships.
  • The QR code appears on the 9:16 Status size only, where there is room; every size carries "hazri.me".

P-cardsThe two event pictures

A Shaheedi day is quiet and dark, with ਕੋਟਿ ਕੋਟਿ ਪ੍ਰਣਾਮ; a Prakash Purab is warm gold, with ਵਧਾਈਆਂ. Each shows the event, the date, one line from its Vichar story, and "Read the full story on Hazri". No picture of a Guru, and no Gurbani on these cards.

  • P-A and P-B go together; the question is whether you want both or only the calendar.
  • The one-line summary comes from the Vichar story's own published text, never written in Dart.
